# Event Presentation

## Outcome And Intake

Help a live audience retain one central idea and act on a clear invitation. Ask for audience familiarity, talk duration, venue/display conditions, expected takeaway, permitted stories, demonstrations, evidence, interaction format and language. An unknown duration or claim remains unresolved; do not fabricate a customer story, quotation, result or endorsement.

## Claim

Each headline is one complete sentence that advances the talk. Favor a concrete observation or useful distinction over a topic label or motivational slogan. The speaker may explain nuance orally, but the slide cannot contradict or overstate the source. The governing message should be repeatable without the speaker's notes.

## Logic

Use an opening question or tension, stakes, explanation, evidence or demonstration, implications, and a closing action. The ledger records the headline, role in the narrative, transition, expected speaking time, evidence, and audience action. Time allocations are authored assumptions, not measured audience behavior. Keep backup details outside the primary narrative.

## Purpose Patterns

| Intent | Required inputs | Native starting point | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frame the central idea | Audience question and answer | list-horizontal/focus | One message, no dense agenda disguised as a cover |
| Contrast a misconception | Exact old/new claims and evidence | before-after/balanced | Same positions and scope; old view is not caricatured |
| Explain a journey | Meaningful stages and transitions | flow/balanced | Stages are large, labeled and legible from the back |
| Show a reinforcing loop | Actual feedback relationship | cycle/balanced | Loop direction is clear; no invented causality |
| Present memorable evidence | One sourced quantity and denominator | horizontal-bar-graph/labeled | Display values are readable and not stripped of context |
| Explain alternatives | Two or three criteria and options | matrix/labeled | Comparison remains readable on a projected slide |
| Show an architecture | Only components needed for the point | diagram/custom | Fewer visible elements, boundaries explained in notes |
| Close with action | Specific invitation, prerequisites, resource | list-horizontal/labeled | Audience can name the next step and its limits |

Use real, licensed assets only when they carry information. This compiler produces native diagrams; it does not invent event photographs, demos, logos or media. More elaborate visual scenes require separately supplied assets and validation.

## Draw

Use substantially larger body text than a reading deck. Prefer at least 22 core units for key labels on a 1280 x 720 canvas and simplify content instead of shrinking it. Keep secondary detail in notes or a separate handout. High contrast, strong hierarchy and generous spacing matter more than decorative animation. Never rely on color alone to distinguish a key category.

Choose a small number of visible elements, but do not make their count the headline. Establish the visual focus before a live reveal. If animation is not supported in the generated PPTX, use separate, stable states and do not claim an animated sequence. Use explicit textual transitions instead of a dense arrow web.

## Evidence And Unknowns

Preserve the source's population, period, uncertainty, and distinction between an actual outcome and an illustration. Keep a readable citation on the page and full reference in notes. An anecdote is not a population estimate. Unknown numbers stay `xx`; avoid a chart if the quantity itself is unavailable.

## Measure And Review

Read the headlines as a spoken narrative. Test whether each slide can be understood at a glance and whether the speaker can transition without introducing a hidden premise. Rehearse timing; a nominal per-slide schedule is only a plan. Check projected-size legibility, label clipping, contrast, images, and native PPTX rendering. Automation cannot certify audience comprehension or speaking performance.

## Delivery And Refinements

Deliver the editable presentation plus concise speaker notes when requested. Offer a tighter time-bound version, a stronger demonstration, or an audience-specific opening. Preserve factual caveats rather than moving them out of sight solely for impact.
